Glide Guide: Turn a Spreadsheet Into an App

Glide is a no-code platform that turns data — a spreadsheet, a Glide Table, or another connected source — into a working app with lists, forms, and user sign-in, without writing code. It is best known for data-driven mobile apps and internal business tools.

Two products with the same name: GlideOS and Glide Classic

Glide now sells two separate products, and this confuses many buyers. GlideOS is the newer AI builder: you describe an app or point it at a spreadsheet and it generates the app, on its own subscription. Glide Classic is the original no-code builder with the long-standing plan ladder. They are separate subscriptions — a Glide Classic plan does not include GlideOS. If you are unsure which one a pricing page is showing, check whether it mentions credits and AI generation (GlideOS) or editors, updates, and users (Glide Classic).

Who Glide is for

  • Small teams that need an internal tool, tracker, or directory quickly.
  • People whose data already lives in Google Sheets or Excel.
  • Builders who want a mobile-friendly app without hiring a developer.

How to get started

  1. Create a free account on the official Glide website.
  2. Connect your data or start from a template.
  3. Choose screens and layouts for your lists and details.
  4. Add sign-in if only certain people should use the app.
  5. Publish and share the link; upgrade only when you need more published apps, users, or updates.

Strengths and limits

Strengths: very fast from spreadsheet to working app; good mobile experience; templates cover common internal tools.

Limits: two product lines make pricing confusing; costs scale with users, rows, and updates, so a busy app can outgrow the entry plans; deeply custom design or logic may need a more flexible builder or code.
